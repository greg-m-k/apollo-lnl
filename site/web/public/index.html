Lunch & Learn

A Lunch & Learn is a format, and this page is an invitation to copy it. Take one design question your team keeps arguing about, build each competing answer for real, run them side by side, and let people find out for themselves. Nothing here is mocked: every router, service, database, queue and consumer below is really running, and every number on screen is measured while you watch. The point is to learn by doing: run it, slow a service down, switch one off, time it, and see what each pattern costs instead of taking a slide's word for it. Then build one of your own on a question your team cares about. The code for this one is on GitHub; fork it as a starting point.

🧩 Federated Services vs Event-Driven Projections

Most companies split their software by domain: one service owns people, another owns jobs, another owns security badges, each with its own team and its own database. That keeps the teams independent, but a single screen often needs data from several of them at once, and no one service has the whole answer. There are two common patterns for putting the answer together. Federated services ask every owning service at the moment of the request and combine the replies. Event-driven projections have each service announce its changes as events, keep a combined copy up to date from those events, and answer from the copy. These lessons run both against the same data and show what each one costs in speed, freshness and failure.
